Sometimes a children's book says far more than many people expect.

I'm the daughter of migrants. I'm the granddaughter of migrants too, and the
great-granddaughter, depending on how you look at it. I grew up in a Galician
village that is still *home*. But things always happened there — and this book
went straight to the heart, to the most migrant part of all of us, and to me.

_"In a little blue town where nothing ever happened"_ catches your eye, but it
reaches your soul. One day someone — or "something" red — arrives in that little
blue town. And because no one accepts it right away, it goes to live in the
abandoned bell tower. Don't imagine the rest, because it may be far less
fanciful than you're picturing, but illustrated as beautifully as the very best.

A story we can all connect to someone we know — or someone we could get to know
a little better, with how close they are to us.
